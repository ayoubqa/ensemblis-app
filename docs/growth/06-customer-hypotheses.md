# Customer hypotheses — who to learn from first

Everything below is a **hypothesis**. There are no customers, interviews, conversion data or payments behind
it yet; where evidence is missing, this document says so rather than filling the gap. Its purpose is to make
the next four weeks of learning cheap and decisive.

## What we can and can't say today

- **Can:** the product runs end to end — sign-up → define an outcome → plan with its cost → approval → research
  and analysis with numbered evidence → verification → report. Verified locally on the test AI provider, in about
  20 seconds, on 29 October 2026.
- **Can't:** how good the output is on real questions with the production model and real web search, whether
  anyone returns, or whether anyone would pay. Those three unknowns decide the business; nothing on the website
  can answer them.

## Segment A — early-stage B2B founders without a research or strategy hire

| | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Founders of B2B companies with roughly 2–20 people, making market, competitor, ICP or pricing decisions themselves. |
| **Problem** | Decisions that deserve a week of research get an evening of searching, because nobody else can do it. |
| **Current alternative** | General AI assistants and their "deep research" modes, search, spreadsheets, advisors; occasionally a freelancer. |
| **Reason to try** | A plan and a cost before anything runs; success criteria they define; every claim numbered to a source; a verification step that says what wasn't established — a document they can forward to a co-founder or investor. |
| **First meaningful outcome** | A competitor map of their 5–10 closest alternatives with one opening per competitor, or a ranked shortlist of markets — in one session. |
| **Use case** | Competitor Analysis + Positioning; Market Research. |
| **Journal topics** | Checking AI research (#3), positioning (#6), ICP from evidence (#9), market entry (#16), the pre-mortem (#5). |
| **Strongest objection** | *"ChatGPT or Perplexity deep research does this for a monthly subscription I already pay."* The answer can't be a claim on our website; it has to be visible in the output: criteria met or not, claims tied to sources that exist, and an honest list of gaps. If the outputs aren't clearly more trustworthy side by side, this segment won't pay. |
| **Willingness-to-pay evidence** | **None yet.** To collect: after a useful result, ask what they would have paid for it and whether they'd pay that for the next one — with a concrete price the founder chooses beforehand. |

## Segment B — small agencies and independent consultants doing client research

| | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Marketing, growth and strategy agencies and independent consultants (1–15 people) who research a client's market, competitors or customers at the start of an engagement. |
| **Problem** | The research is necessary and often under-billed; it has to be sourced well enough to put in front of a client. |
| **Current alternative** | Junior staff time, the same AI assistants, manual desk research, existing SEO and market tools. |
| **Reason to try** | A repeatable brief per client with sources the client can check, and a predictable cost per job they can price into a proposal. |
| **First meaningful outcome** | A client competitor audit they adapt into a deliverable (export to PDF or Word already exists). |
| **Use case** | Competitor Analysis, Market Research, Positioning; Account Research for business development. |
| **Journal topics** | Competitor research to positioning (#12), positioning (#6), citable content (#14), GEO without the myths (#1) — agencies are being asked about AI search right now. |
| **Strongest objection** | *"Can I put my name on AI-made work, and will it be consistent from one client to the next?"* Again only answered by outputs, and by being clear about what the verification does and doesn't confirm. |
| **Willingness-to-pay evidence** | **None yet.** This segment already buys tools for client work, which suggests a budget line exists — but that is an inference, not evidence about Ensemblis. |

**Why both, for now:** A fits the product as it is and the people the site already speaks to; B has the more
natural repeat use (one run per client) and an existing habit of paying for tools. Four weeks of conversations
should tell which one returns without being asked.

## One distinctive marketing asset

**"The research brief" — a one-page template and checklist**, free, usable with any tool (including the
competitors'): the decision being made, the question, the success criteria, the sources an answer must cite,
what would change your mind — and the five checks from article #3 on the back. It is useful on its own, it
teaches exactly the habit Ensemblis is built around, and it gives problem-solving articles a natural,
non-pushy next step. Make it once the founder approves article #3; it needs no new infrastructure (a PDF in
`/public` linked from the article, or a Journal page).

## The fastest path to learning: concierge runs

For four weeks, instead of waiting for traffic:

1. **Recruit 15–20 people** across both segments from the founder's own network and communities (no paid
   acquisition). Offer one real question, run together.
2. **Run it with them** on production (real model, real web search) in a 30-minute call: they write the objective
   and criteria; they watch the plan, the cost and the result arrive. Record what confused them.
3. **Ask three questions afterwards:** Would you act on this? What would you have done without it, and what would
   that have cost? Would you pay [the founder's chosen price] for the next one?
4. **Wait.** Count who comes back on their own within two weeks (`funnel.sql`, `came_back_another_week`).
5. **Decide with a bar you set before you start** — for example: if fewer than a quarter of participants act on
   their result, fix output quality before any marketing; if several return unprompted and accept the price, put
   that segment's use case first on the homepage and in the editorial plan.

This doubles as the only honest quality check available: the first real runs show whether the output is good
enough to sell, and they produce the genuine examples the two product-led articles (#12, #16) need.

## What not to build yet

- No new landing pages per segment until the concierge runs show which one returns.
- No paid acquisition before a result that people act on.
- No new features to answer the "deep research" objection until the side-by-side shows where Ensemblis is
  actually better or worse.
