# Editorial plan — first 30 days (2–27 November 2026)

## Strategy in one paragraph

The Journal earns attention from marketers and founders asking how AI answer engines are changing discovery,
and converts the ones who need research they can trust. Ensemblis does **not** measure AI-search visibility,
so the Journal must never imply it; its honest bridge to the product is the discipline both share —
defined criteria, cited evidence, verification — and the research work behind content and positioning
decisions (competitors, customers, markets), which Ensemblis does today. Topics are chosen for search
intent, for whether we can add something a generic summary can't (a framework, a verified comparison,
real data), and for a natural next step into the product.

## What this plan does and doesn't claim

- **No search-volume or difficulty numbers.** None were measured; the drafting environment had no keyword tool.
  The "Research needed" line under each article says what to check (Search Console once verified, a keyword
  tool, the live search results) before writing. Treat the plan as hypotheses to validate, not a promise of demand.
- Sources marked in the drafts were found through search excerpts; editors must open and confirm them.

**Mix over the month:** educational 6 (38%), practical guides 5 (31%), measurement and frameworks 3 (19%),
product-led 2 (12%) — close to the 40/30/20/10 starting point. Adjust after four weeks of Search Console and
`blog_cta_click` data.

**Cadence.** Four a week is the ambition. Six of these sixteen need data you don't have yet (Search Console,
a real Ensemblis execution, vendor documentation read in full). Our recommendation: publish the two drafted
pillars in week 1, hold weeks 1–2 at **two to three** articles while the review step is tested, and reach four
in weeks 3–4 only if every article passes the editor's checklist. A missed slot costs nothing; a weak
article costs trust.

## The articles

Each entry: **audience · search intent · primary topic · what makes it worth reading · internal links ·
next step (CTA) · research needed before writing.**

### 1. What is generative engine optimization (GEO)? — *drafted*
- **Audience:** marketers and founders hearing "GEO" from vendors. **Intent:** informational (definition).
- **Topic:** generative engine optimization / what is GEO.
- **Value:** the term's origin and what the 40% figure really measured; Google's "no special optimization" stance; the promises to distrust.
- **Links:** #2, #3, #5, /use-cases. **CTA:** use-cases.
- **Research:** open every source; replace secondary coverage of the SparkToro study with the original report.

### 2. The AI Performance Funnel — *drafted*
- **Audience:** marketing leads asked to report on "AI visibility". **Intent:** framework / how to measure.
- **Topic:** measuring brand visibility in AI answers; recommendation rate.
- **Value:** exact definitions, Recommendation Conversion, margin-of-error maths, written as a proposal with limits.
- **Links:** #1, #6, #7, #14, /how-it-works. **CTA:** how-it-works.
- **Research:** confirm sources; decide on the ™; consider publishing the prompt-set template from #6 alongside.
