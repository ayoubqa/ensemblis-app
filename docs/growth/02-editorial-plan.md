# Editorial plan — first 30 days (2–27 November 2026)

*Revised in Phase 2: the same sixteen slots, broadened from a search-marketing calendar (13 of 16 articles
about SEO/GEO) into a business publication. What changed and why is at the end.*

## Strategy in one paragraph

The Journal is a publication for people who run small businesses and teams — founders, operators, marketing
and sales leads — about the work itself: putting AI to work, strategy, marketing, sales, search and operations.
It earns attention by being useful to readers who may never buy anything, and earns trust by sourcing what it
claims. Ensemblis does **not** measure AI-search visibility, so the Journal must never imply it; its honest
bridges to the product are the research jobs Ensemblis does today (markets, competitors, customers, accounts,
processes, scenarios) and the discipline both share — defined criteria, cited evidence, verification.

## Three editorial layers

Every article declares why it exists (`layer` in its frontmatter — see `03-publishing.md`):

| Layer | Purpose | Call to action | Share of this plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Broad ideas and analysis a reader would share; builds an audience | None by default | 4 of 16 (25%) |
| **Problem-solving** | Practical help with a problem our readers have | A soft, relevant next step (a use case, the walkthrough, pricing) | 10 of 16 (62%) |
| **Product-led** | A real job done with Ensemblis, shown as it ran | Try it on your own question | 2 of 16 (13%) |

A product-led article exists only when a real execution backs it. Problem-solving articles mention Ensemblis
only where it genuinely fits, and never in the middle of the advice.

## What this plan does and doesn't claim

- **No search-volume, traffic or conversion numbers.** None were measured; the drafting environment had no
  keyword tool and the site has no analytics data yet. The "Research" line under each article says what to check
  before writing. Treat every topic as a hypothesis about what readers want.
- **Evidence notes name sources to check, not facts to repeat.** Where a study is named below, the writer must
  open it and quote it from the original; numbers seen only in summaries do not go into an article.
- Sources in the two drafts were found through search excerpts; editors must open and confirm them.

**Cadence.** Four a week is the ambition. Four of these sixteen need something we don't have yet (two real
Ensemblis executions; a template tested on a real category; a positioning example we may publish). Our
recommendation: publish the two drafted articles in week 1, hold weeks 1–2 at **two to three** articles while the
review step is tested, and reach four in weeks 3–4 only if every article passes the editor's checklist. A
missed slot costs nothing; a weak article costs trust.

## The articles

Each entry: **audience · reader's question · what makes it worth reading · internal links · next step ·
evidence and research before writing.**

### 1. What is generative engine optimization (GEO)? — *drafted, in review*
- **Audience:** marketers and founders hearing "GEO" from vendors. **Question:** what is it, and what can it promise?
- **Value:** the term's origin and what the 40% figure measured; Google's "no additional requirements" stance; an original crawl-to-citation diagram; the promises to distrust.
- **Links:** #2, #7, #14, /use-cases. **Next step:** use-cases.
- **Evidence:** sources re-checked through search excerpts on 28 Oct; primary Google and Microsoft posts replaced secondary coverage. Editors must open each before approving (see the article's `editorNotes`).

### 2. The AI Performance Funnel — *drafted, in review*
- **Audience:** marketing leads asked to report on "AI visibility". **Question:** how do we measure this honestly?
- **Value:** exact definitions, Recommendation Conversion, margin-of-error maths — written as a proposal with its limits.
- **Links:** #1, #10, /how-it-works. **Next step:** how-it-works.
- **Evidence:** as #1. Decide on the ™ before publishing. The framework stays labelled as a proposal, not a standard.

### 3. How to check AI-generated business research before you act on it
- **Audience:** managers receiving AI-made analysis (from a tool, a vendor or a colleague). **Question:** how do I know whether to trust this?
- **Value:** a five-check routine — does it answer the question asked, does each claim have a source, do the sources exist and say that, what is missing, does it contradict itself — with a worked example on a public topic.
- **Links:** #8, /how-it-works#verification. **Next step:** how-it-works.
- **Evidence:** Walters & Wilder, "Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT", *Scientific Reports* 13, 14045 (2023) — fabricated and erroneous citations by model version; NIST AI 600-1, *Generative AI Profile* (July 2024) — "confabulation" as a named risk. Open both; quote from the originals.

### 4. What an AI agent is — and isn't
- **Audience:** managers being sold "agents". **Question:** what does the word actually mean, and what should I ask a vendor?
- **Value:** a plain definition (a model that plans and uses tools in steps toward a goal), the spectrum from chat to autonomous, and five questions to ask before trusting one with work — approvals, tools, cost limits, evidence, failure handling.
- **Links:** #3, #8. **Next step:** none (discovery).
- **Evidence:** how major providers define agents in their own documentation (quote, date and link each); no market-size or adoption statistics unless from a primary survey.

### 5. The pre-mortem
- **Audience:** founders and team leads before a big decision. **Question:** how do we catch the flaw in a plan before we commit?
- **Value:** the technique in one page — imagine it failed, list why, act on the top reasons — with a template.
- **Links:** #16. **Next step:** none (discovery).
- **Evidence:** Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem", *Harvard Business Review* (September 2007) — the original description; cite it, don't paraphrase beyond it.

### 6. Positioning for small teams
- **Audience:** founders writing their own homepage. **Question:** how do we say what we are and who it's for?
- **Value:** a working method: real alternatives customers consider → what's uniquely true of us → who cares most → the category we compete in; with a before/after on a fictional, labelled example.
- **Links:** #9, #12, /use-cases#marketing. **Next step:** use-cases.
- **Evidence:** credit established methods by name (e.g. April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*, 2019) and describe them accurately; no invented case studies.

### 7. AI crawlers explained: what to allow, what to block
- **Audience:** site owners and developers. **Question:** which AI bots should our robots.txt allow?
- **Value:** a decision matrix by goal (appear in AI search vs. opt out of training vs. user-triggered fetches), tested robots.txt examples, CDN caveats.
- **Links:** #1, #14. **Next step:** how-it-works.
- **Evidence:** each vendor's current crawler documentation (names change — OpenAI's moved to developers.openai.com in 2026); test every example against a robots.txt parser.

### 8. Where generative AI helps at work — and where it hurts
- **Audience:** anyone deciding where to use AI. **Question:** what does the evidence say, beyond anecdotes?
- **Value:** a careful reading of the few field and controlled studies: who gained, on which tasks, and where quality fell.
- **Links:** #3, #4. **Next step:** none (discovery).
- **Evidence:** Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work", *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140(2), 2025 (first NBER working paper 31161, 2023 — the headline figures differ between the two versions: quote the published one); Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (Harvard Business School working paper 24-013, 2023; check for the later journal version). Quote effect sizes only from the papers, with their caveats.

### 9. Your ideal customer profile, built from evidence
- **Audience:** founders and first sales hires. **Question:** who should we sell to first?
- **Value:** an ICP built from closed deals, lost deals and conversations — with a one-page template and the mistakes that make ICPs useless.
- **Links:** #6, #13, /use-cases#marketing. **Next step:** use-cases.
- **Evidence:** mostly method; any statistic about ICP effectiveness must come from a primary study or be left out.

### 10. Why one screenshot proves nothing
- **Audience:** marketers presenting AI-visibility results. **Question:** how many prompts and runs do we need?
- **Value:** variance explained plainly; a margin-of-error table; a sampling plan.
- **Links:** #2, #1. **Next step:** how-it-works.
- **Evidence:** the SparkToro/Gumshoe report (January 2026) from SparkToro's own site, and any other published consistency studies.

### 11. What should a research task cost?
- **Audience:** operators commissioning research (to a freelancer, agency, employee or tool). **Question:** how do I estimate it before I commit?
- **Value:** a way to scope a task (questions, sources, depth, verification) and estimate time and cost for each option — with Ensemblis's credit costs shown as one option, from the pricing page.
- **Links:** /pricing, #3. **Next step:** pricing.
- **Evidence:** no invented market rates. If quoting what agencies or freelancers charge, use a published, dated source or leave the number to the reader.

### 12. From competitor research to positioning (product-led)
- **Audience:** founders and marketing leads. **Question:** how do I go from "who are our competitors" to a positioning decision?
- **Value:** a real Ensemblis execution on public companies — plan, cost, evidence, verification — shown as it ran, including what it couldn't establish.
- **Links:** #6, /use-cases#marketing. **Next step:** research-brief.
- **Evidence:** run it in production with the real model and web search; show only what it produced.

### 13. Before the first sales call
- **Audience:** founders and account executives. **Question:** what should I know about this account before I call?
- **Value:** a thirty-minute research routine — company, priorities, recent events, likely needs, one conversation angle — and what not to say from public information.
- **Links:** #9, /use-cases#sales. **Next step:** use-cases.
- **Evidence:** method only; public sources named by type (filings, press releases, job postings), not by unverified claims about their reliability.

### 14. What makes content citable?
- **Audience:** content writers and editors. **Question:** what makes a page easy for people and AI systems to quote accurately?
- **Value:** separates practices with evidence (citations, statistics, quotations in the GEO paper's experiments) from speculation, labelled as such.
- **Links:** #1, #7. **Next step:** research-brief.
- **Evidence:** the GEO paper's method and Table 1 (quote numbers only from it); Google's guidance on AI features.

### 15. Map it before you automate it
- **Audience:** operations leads and founders tempted to automate. **Question:** which process should we automate, and how do we avoid automating a mess?
- **Value:** a lightweight process review — steps, owners, hand-offs, failure points — and a test for whether a step is worth automating.
- **Links:** #11, /use-cases#operations. **Next step:** none (discovery).
- **Evidence:** method only; no automation ROI figures unless from a primary, dated study.

### 16. Running a market-entry analysis with cited evidence (product-led)
- **Audience:** founders weighing expansion. **Question:** how do we choose which market to enter next?
- **Value:** a real execution from objective to verified result, with cost, evidence and stated limits.
- **Links:** #5, /use-cases#marketing, /pricing. **Next step:** research-brief.
- **Evidence:** run it in production on a public, non-sensitive example; show the actual output only.

## How we will judge the plan (after four weeks)

Per layer, not per article — four weeks of a new site is too little data for article-level conclusions:

- **Discovery:** organic visits, `article_read_complete` rate, returning readers.
- **Problem-solving:** read-complete rate and `blog_related_click` (the section's next step).
- **Product-led:** `blog_cta_click` → access request or sign-up → first objective.

See `04-measurement.md` for the events and what they can't tell us.

## What changed in this revision, and why

- **Breadth.** Search-focused articles went from 13 of 16 to 5 of 16. The Journal now covers six sections;
  the founder brief asked for a business publication, and a narrow SEO calendar limits who finds Ensemblis.
- **Layers.** Each article declares discovery, problem-solving or product-led; discovery articles carry no
  product call to action.
- **Evidence notes.** Topics that invite statistics (AI productivity, automation ROI, research costs) name the
  primary sources to check — or say to leave numbers out.
- **Kept:** the two drafted articles, the AI crawlers guide, the screenshot/sampling article, the citable-content
  checklist, both product-led walkthroughs, the dates and the cadence advice.
